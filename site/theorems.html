Theorems and Results

All results from the FMF framework, grouped by topic. Status badges indicate proof level: Proved Empirical Open

Claim boundary: this page summarizes density and reduction results. It does not claim a proof of the Collatz conjecture. The missing pointwise bridge is the unweighted terminal-window/rank theorem described in the paper and repository audit notes.

FMF Formulas

Theorem A (FMF Step Formula) Proved

Let \(x = 4k + 3\) with \(k\) odd, and write \(k + 1 = 2^t m\) where \(m\) is odd and \(t \geq 1\). Then the standard Collatz trajectory from \(x\) reaches the next multiple of four in exactly

\[\text{steps}(x) = 3 + 2t\]

and the FMF image is

\[F(x) = 2\bigl(3^{t+2}\,m - 1\bigr)\]

The parameter \(t = v_2(k+1)\) completely determines the step count and multiplicative growth factor \(3^{t+2}/2^{3+2t}\).

Lemma (\(v_2\) of FMF) Proved

For the FMF image \(F(x) = 2(3^{t+2}m - 1)\), the 2-adic valuation of \(F(x)\) satisfies

\[v_2\bigl(F(x)\bigr) = 1 + v_2\bigl(m - \mathrm{inv}_{t+2}\bigr)\]

where \(\mathrm{inv}_{n}\) denotes the multiplicative inverse of \(3^n\) modulo \(2^K\) for sufficiently large \(K\). This links the 2-adic structure of outputs to modular inverses.

Lemma (\(v_2(3^n - 1)\)) Proved

For all \(n \geq 1\),

\[ v_2(3^n - 1)= \begin{cases} 1, & n \text{ odd},\\ v_2(n)+2, & n \text{ even}. \end{cases} \]

This classical result follows from the Lifting the Exponent Lemma applied to \(3^n - 1^n\).

State Independence

Theorem B (State Independence) Proved

The FMF output-state distribution is independent of the input state in natural density. This is a statement about residue classes over many starts, not independence along a single deterministic trajectory.

In the density model, the relevant \(2\)-adic valuations have the expected geometric law.

Observation (Drift) Proved

The expected logarithmic drift per FMF step is

\[\mathbb{E}\bigl[\log_2 R\bigr] = \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^t} \log_2\!\left(\frac{3^{t+2}}{2^{3+2t}}\right) = -0.830 \text{ bits/hop}\]

On average, each FMF application reduces the number by 0.830 bits. This is a provable consequence of the exact transition probabilities.

Contraction

Theorem C (Fractional-Moment Contraction) Proved

For the FMF hop ratio \(R=F(x)/x\), the exact asymptotic natural-density moment \(M(\alpha)=\mathbb{E}[R^\alpha]\) satisfies

\[M(0.53) \approx 0.8638 < 1.\]

The critical exponent solving \(M(\alpha^*)=1\) is approximately \(\alpha^* \approx 1.0002\). This is a density-model fractional-moment contraction statement, not a pointwise Lyapunov function for every trajectory.

Lemma (3/4 Discriminant) Proved

The number 3 is the largest odd integer less than 4. Consequently, for the Collatz map \(x \mapsto 3x+1\), the ratio \(3/4 < 1\) after removing two factors of 2 guarantees that the "minimum" FMF step (\(t=1\)) is already contracting:

\[\frac{3^3}{2^5} = \frac{27}{32} < 1\]

For any "\(qx+1\)" map with \(q \geq 5\) odd, this discriminant fails and cycles can appear.

Lemma (Inverse Movement) Proved

The sequence of 2-adic inverses \(\mathrm{inv}_t = (3^t)^{-1} \bmod 2^K\) satisfies

\[v_2(\mathrm{inv}_{t+1} - \mathrm{inv}_t) = v_2(1 - a)\]

where \(a\) is the 2-adic inverse of 3. This controls how quickly the "alignment windows" for high \(v_2\) outputs shift as \(t\) increases.

Quartering Law

Theorem D (Quartering Law) Proved

In the FMF framework, define a growth chain as a maximal consecutive sequence of FMF steps where the output exceeds the input. Conditioned on a growth-B hop, the next hop is again growth-B with density

\[\mathbb{P}(\text{continue}\mid\text{growth-B}) = \frac{1}{4}\]

Equivalently, among all odd starts, strict growth-B chains of length at least \(k\) have natural density \(4^{-k}\). The older expression \(2/4^k\) is only the conditional normalization after the start is already known to be Type B.

Proposition (2-adic Expansion) Proved

Each growth step in the FMF map expands the 2-adic representation by a factor of at least

\[8\]

This helps explain the density decay, but it does not by itself prove pointwise termination of every growth chain.

Lemma (Growth-B Acyclicity) Proved

The periodic growth-B branch families have no positive cycles: a cycle would force an equality \(3^a = 2^b\) in the relevant multiplier equation. This excludes periodic obstructions, but it does not exclude arbitrary nonperiodic growth-B depth sequences.

Growth Bounds

Observation (Epoch Duration) Empirical

Computationally, the number of FMF steps in a complete epoch (from an integer to a smaller integer) satisfies

\[\text{epoch}(x) \leq 2.83 \cdot \log_2(x)\]

for the tested ranges. This is empirical evidence only; a universal epoch bound would be a pointwise theorem strong enough to imply Collatz by descent.

Observation (Growth Phase Structure) Empirical

In computational experiments up to \(2^{60}\), no growth chain exceeds 7 consecutive FMF hops. The Quartering Law predicts \(\mathbb{P}(\text{chain} \geq 7) = 2/4^7 \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-4}\), consistent with observations.

Conjecture (Growth Chain Bound) Open

For all odd \(m\), the length of the longest growth chain starting from \(m\) satisfies

\[\text{chain}(m) \leq 0.55 \cdot \log_2(m) + 3\]

If proved in a sufficiently strong pointwise form and combined with recovery after mixed growth and shrinkage phases, this would materially strengthen the FMF route to Collatz.

Main Result

Main Theorem (Density and Reduction Package) Proved

Let \(E_k(N) = \{x \leq N : x \text{ has a growth chain of length} \geq k\}\). Then

\[\limsup_{N \to \infty} \frac{|E_k(N)|}{N} \leq 4^{-k}\]

This is a natural-density statement about strict growth-B chains, together with exact reductions of the first bounded-depth pointwise obstructions to return-code and reduced-gap partial maps. It should not be compared as a replacement for Tao's orbit-minimum theorem, because the measured events are different.

  • Binary return-code survival density: \((2/15)^L\)
  • Reduced \(\{0,1\}\) gap-code survival density: \(112^{-L}\)
  • Remaining target: an unweighted terminal-window/rank theorem.
