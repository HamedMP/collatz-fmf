First-Multiple-of-Four Coordinates for Collatz Growth Chains: Density Bounds and Pointwise Obstruction Reductions

Hamed Mohammadpour

Abstract

The First-Multiple-of-Four (FMF) decomposition partitions Collatz dynamics into odd-to-odd hops ending at the first value divisible by four. In these coordinates the hop length and output value have exact algebraic formulas, the output states have natural-density laws obtained from 2-adic inverse structure, and the FMF hop ratio has an exact asymptotic fractional-moment contraction \(\mathbb{E}[R^{0.53}] \approx 0.8638 < 1\) in the density model.

The main density result is the Quartering Law: conditioned on a growth-B hop, each further growth-B continuation has density factor \(1/4\), so the density among odd starts of a strict growth-B chain of length at least \(k\) is \(4^{-k}\). The paper also gives pointwise reductions for the first bounded-depth obstruction classes: binary-depth return obstructions reduce to an explicit return-code partial map with survival density \((2/15)^L\), while the reduced \(\{0,1\}\) gap obstruction has survival density \(112^{-L}\).

These are density and reduction theorems for isolated Collatz growth mechanisms, not a proof of the Collatz conjecture and not a replacement for Tao's orbit-minimum theorem. The remaining pointwise gap is an unweighted terminal-window/rank problem in \(2\)-adic dynamics.

This is a density-and-reduction manuscript, not a claimed proof of the Collatz conjecture.
