First-Multiple-of-Four Coordinates for Collatz Growth Chains

Density bounds and pointwise obstruction reductions

Hamed Mohammadpour

Abstract

This paper introduces the First-Multiple-of-Four (FMF) decomposition, which partitions Collatz trajectories into odd-to-odd hops through the first value divisible by four. The decomposition gives exact algebraic formulas for hop length and output value, natural-density output laws from 2-adic inverse structure, and an exact asymptotic fractional moment contraction \(\mathbb{E}[R^{0.53}] \approx 0.8638 < 1\) for the FMF hop ratio.

The main density result is the Quartering Law: among starts already in a growth-B hop, each additional growth-B continuation has density factor \(1/4\), so the density among odd starts of a strict growth-B chain of length at least \(k\) is \(4^{-k}\). The paper also gives exact pointwise obstruction reductions for the first bounded-depth cases: binary-depth return obstructions reduce to a 2-adic return-code partial map with survival density \((2/15)^L\), while the reduced \(\{0,1\}\) gap obstruction has survival density \(112^{-L}\).

These are density and reduction theorems for isolated Collatz growth mechanisms. They are not a proof of the Collatz conjecture and not a replacement for Tao's orbit-minimum theorem. The remaining pointwise gap is stated explicitly as an unweighted terminal-window/rank problem in \(p\)-adic dynamics.

Key Results

Theorem A: FMF Step Formula

For any odd \(x = 4k+3\) with \(k\) odd, write \(k + 1 = 2^t m\) where \(m\) is odd. Then the Collatz trajectory from \(x\) reaches the next multiple of four in exactly

\[\text{steps} = 3 + 2t\]

and the FMF image is

\[F(x) = 2\bigl(3^{t+2}\,m - 1\bigr)\]

Theorem D: The Quartering Law

Conditioned on already being in a growth-B hop, each additional growth-B continuation has density factor

\[\mathbb{P}(\text{continue}) = \frac{1}{4}\]

Among all odd starts, strict growth-B chains of length at least \(k\) have natural density \(4^{-k}\). This is a natural-density statement, not a pointwise stopping theorem.

Pointwise Obstruction Reductions

The first bounded-depth pointwise obstructions are reduced to explicit partial maps and terminal-window counting problems:

  • Binary return-code survival density \((2/15)^L\)
  • Reduced \(\{0,1\}\) gap-code survival density \(112^{-L}\)
  • Periodic, finite-defect, low-complexity, balanced/Sturmian, and several small automatic-template obstruction classes are excluded.

Honest Assessment

What is proved

  • The FMF closed-form formulas (Theorem A), with finite script checks as supporting verification
  • Input-state-independent FMF output distributions in natural density (Theorem B)
  • Fractional-moment contraction \(M(0.53) \approx 0.8638 < 1\) in the density model (Theorem C)
  • The Quartering Law with exact \((1/4)^k\) density decay (Theorem D)
  • Exact binary return-code and reduced-gap obstruction reductions

What is NOT proved

  • Pointwise convergence to 1 for every integer -- the central claim of the Collatz conjecture
  • A universal pointwise bound on all growth phases or mixed recovery epochs
  • That density-zero exceptional sets are empty
  • The unweighted terminal-window/rank theorem needed to close the first bounded-depth obstructions
  • Average contraction does not imply pointwise convergence; this is the main mathematical gap
